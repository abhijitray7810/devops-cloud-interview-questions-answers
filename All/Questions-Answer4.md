31. What is the difference between IaaS, PaaS and SaaS?

Answer:

IaaS: You manage more of the infrastructure, such as virtual machines and operating systems.

PaaS: The provider manages more infrastructure and platform components while you focus more on the application.

SaaS: You primarily consume the finished software.

Consulting angle: Pick the abstraction level that best balances control, speed, risk and operational effort.

32. What is a VNet/VPC?

Answer:

It is a logically isolated network environment in a cloud provider. It allows you to define IP ranges, subnets, routing, security boundaries and connectivity to other networks.

33. What is the difference between a public and private subnet?

Answer:

A public subnet has a route that can provide direct internet connectivity through the appropriate gateway architecture. A private subnet does not expose workloads directly to the internet and typically uses controlled egress or internal connectivity.

34. What is identity and access management?

Answer:

IAM controls who or what can access resources and what actions they are allowed to perform. Good IAM follows least privilege, separation of duties and auditable access patterns.

35. What is least privilege?

Answer:

Give a user, service or workload only the permissions required to perform its intended task, for only the necessary scope and duration.

41. What problem does Docker solve?

42. What problem does Kubernetes solve?

Answer:

“Kubernetes automates scheduling, desired-state management, service discovery, scaling, rollout and recovery for containerized workloads. It helps teams operate distributed applications consistently.”

43. What is a Kubernetes Deployment?

Answer:

“A Deployment manages a replicated set of application pods and provides declarative updates, rollout control and rollback mechanisms.”

44. What is a Kubernetes Service?

Answer:

“A Service provides a stable network endpoint for a set of pods and uses label selection to route traffic to those pods.”

45. A pod keeps restarting. How do you troubleshoot it?

Answer:

“I would check pod status and events, inspect logs including previous-container logs, verify probes, inspect resource requests and limits, review configuration and secrets, and check whether the container process itself is exiting. I would avoid changing several things simultaneously.”

46. What is the difference between liveness and readiness probes?

Answer:

“Readiness determines whether a workload should receive traffic. Liveness determines whether the container should be considered unhealthy enough to restart. Confusing them can cause either unavailable traffic or unnecessary restarts.”

47. How would you design a self-healing Kubernetes platform?

Strong answer:

“I would combine declarative workload management, replicas, health probes, resource controls, automated rollouts, autoscaling where appropriate, robust observability and safe failure handling. Self-healing does not mean hiding failures; it means recovering from known failure modes while still producing signals for operators.”

48. What is CI/CD?

Answer:

“Continuous integration validates changes frequently through automated build and test workflows. Continuous delivery or deployment automates the path toward production, with appropriate approvals, tests and safeguards.”

49. How can CI/CD improve reliability?

Answer:

“By reducing manual variation, making changes smaller and repeatable, catching defects earlier, enforcing policy gates and creating a traceable deployment history.”

50. What would you monitor in a production Kubernetes platform?

Answer:

“I would monitor the golden signals—latency, traffic, errors and saturation—plus pod restarts, resource utilization, deployment health, node conditions, control-plane signals where available, application-specific metrics, logs and distributed traces.”

G. Troubleshooting and case questions

56. Application latency suddenly increases. How do you investigate?

Answer:

“I would first determine scope: all users or a subset, one region or multiple, one endpoint or the whole service. Then I would compare latency, errors and traffic over time, check recent changes, inspect downstream dependencies, database behavior and resource saturation, and use logs/traces to find where time is being spent.”

57. CPU is at 95%. Do you immediately scale out?

Answer:

“No. I first determine whether CPU is actually the bottleneck and whether the increase is expected. I would check request rate, latency, memory, throttling, workload behavior and recent changes. Scaling may help, but it can also hide an inefficient operation or an application defect.”

58. A deployment succeeded but users cannot access the application. What do you check?

Answer:

“I would verify pod readiness, service selectors and endpoints, ingress or load-balancer configuration, DNS, network policies, TLS, application logs and external dependencies. I would trace the request path from client to application rather than assuming the deployment itself is the issue.”

59. A customer's application is healthy but the business KPI is falling. What do you investigate?

Answer:

“That tells me technical health and business health are not identical. I would examine user behavior, funnel conversion, latency by customer segment, errors that may not trigger infrastructure alerts, feature changes, dependency behavior and business events. The consultant's job is to connect technology signals to business outcomes.”

6. Consulting case framework

When given a broad case, use this sequence:

1. Clarify the business goal
2. Understand current state
3. Identify constraints
4. Quantify scale and priorities
5. Identify risks and dependencies
6. Propose 2–3 options
7. Compare cost / security / performance / complexity
8. Recommend one option
9. Define phased implementation
10. Define success metrics
11. Explain rollback / mitigation

Example case

Interviewer: “A retail customer has an on-premises application with seasonal traffic and wants to modernize it.”

Good response:

“I’d first understand the business objective: is the main goal cost, scalability, time to market, resilience or modernization? Then I’d map the application dependencies and data sensitivity. I’d evaluate whether a lift-and-shift, replatform or partial modernization makes sense. I’d propose a pilot, define security and identity requirements, estimate the economics, plan migration waves and define success metrics such as availability, latency, deployment frequency and cost per transaction.”

12. Interview-day rules

Rule 1 — Clarify before answering

Do not rush into architecture. Ask what success means.

Rule 2 — Think aloud

Microsoft's technical guidance says technical interviews are problem-solving based and assess how candidates approach problems, technical agility and strategic thinking. citeturn932270search7

Rule 3 — Admit uncertainty honestly

Say:

“I have not implemented that directly, but I understand the concept. I would approach it by…”

That is stronger than inventing experience.

Rule 4 — Use numbers

Your resume contains useful measurable outcomes: 20% faster per-batch turnaround, 18% lower mislabeled-object rate, 97%+ labeling accuracy and 30% lower manual release effort. Be able to explain exactly how each metric was measured. fileciteturn0file0L13-L21 fileciteturn0file0L30-L34

Rule 5 — Connect technology to outcomes

“I used Terraform to make infrastructure repeatable, reviewable and easier to change consistently.”

Rule 6 — Always discuss trade-offs

A consultant should be able to say why a solution is appropriate and what it costs in complexity, money, risk or operational effort.
